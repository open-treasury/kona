# Component guidance

Load only the sections relevant to the requested component or content category. Explicit constraints
and established product conventions take precedence.

## Titles and headers

- State the subject or outcome early, use sentence case, and keep wording descriptive rather than
  clever.
- Do not end short titles or headers with punctuation unless they are questions or complete sentences
  that require it.

## Buttons and links

- Lead action labels with a specific verb and name the expected action or result.
- Keep labels concise without sacrificing clarity. Distinguish nearby actions and avoid generic
  labels when the destination or consequence matters.
- Make links describe their destination or purpose; do not use "click here" or rely on raw URLs.

## Errors, support, and recovery

- Identify the problem specifically in reader-facing terms. Do not blame the reader or expose
  implementation details.
- Give a useful recovery action when one exists and explain irreversible consequences before the
  action.
- Keep the tone calm and kind. Avoid jokes, false reassurance, and apologies unless the product or
  organization is responsible.

## Confirmations and notifications

- Lead with what happened, what will happen, or what needs attention.
- Include a next action only when the reader can or must act. Distinguish success, warning, and
  failure without relying on color or punctuation alone.
- Avoid unnecessary exclamation marks, repeated status words, and vague urgency.

## Empty states and hint text

- Explain what is absent and, when useful, why it matters or how to begin.
- Make the next action concrete. Do not blame the reader or fill space with promotional language.
- Hint text should clarify format, constraints, or consequences rather than repeat a label.

## Alt text

- Describe the image's purpose and essential information in context.
- Use empty alt text for decorative images when the source system supports that semantic choice.
- Do not duplicate nearby text, include visual trivia, or begin with "image of."

## Marketing

- Lead with a supported reader benefit and a clear action. Keep claims specific, qualified, and
  grounded in supplied evidence.
- Use personality sparingly. Do not manufacture scarcity, certainty, social proof, comparisons, or
  performance claims.
