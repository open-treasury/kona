# Style and safety

Apply these defaults only after explicit user requirements and authoritative repository or product
conventions.

## Voice and clarity

- Default to concise American English, plain conversational language, active voice, and sentence
  case.
- Address the reader as `you` when appropriate. Use natural contractions and verb-led action labels.
- Prefer familiar, specific words and consistent terminology. Remove repetition, jargon, empty
  marketing language, and explanations the reader does not need.
- Preserve necessary meaning, empathy, qualifications, and recovery information when shortening.
- Ground statements in the brief or authoritative local evidence. Do not invent facts, metrics,
  testimonials, guarantees, urgency, affiliation, or product capabilities.

## Contextual tone

- Product and operational copy is calm, direct, and useful.
- Support, negative, sensitive, and error copy is kind, specific, non-blaming, and actionable, with
  little or no humor.
- Apologize only when the product or organization is responsible, and make the apology sincere.
- Marketing may use restrained personality or wit when the audience and brand permit it, but clarity
  and truthful claims take priority.
- Avoid exclamation marks and excessive punctuation. Favor descriptive names over clever ones.

## Accessibility, inclusion, and localization

- Use inclusive language that does not assume identity, ability, family structure, culture, or
  technical knowledge unless the context establishes it.
- Avoid idioms, slang, directional wording, and sensory-only instructions when clearer wording is
  available.
- Make link text descriptive without relying on surrounding text or generic phrases such as "click
  here" or "learn more."
- Write alt text for the image's purpose in context. Do not repeat adjacent text or begin with
  "image of."
- Keep wording localization-ready: use complete thoughts, avoid ambiguous pronouns and fragments,
  and do not concatenate sentence parts.
- Defer exact date, number, and currency formats to established product or runtime locale
  conventions. Do not hard-code a format when no convention is established.

## Review classification and final checks

Classify objective violations of requirements, meaning, accuracy, accessibility, inclusion,
localization readiness, or established terminology as required corrections. Classify taste-based
wording preferences as optional suggestions.

Before returning copy, check every explicit constraint, intended meaning, terminology, claim, tone,
next action, accessibility and inclusion, localization readiness, duplication, and unresolved
placeholder. Prefer one recommended version unless a small labeled set materially helps.
